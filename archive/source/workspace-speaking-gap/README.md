# Sayla carousel — "The speaking gap"

Built from Tobia's carousel-designer method: four questions in, covers tested, one CTA, scored before shipping.
Article: *How to Mass Produce Slideshows to 10X Your App Revenue* (X, 2026-11-02). His skill is email-gated;
this is a local implementation of the method, not his files.

## The four questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What are you selling? | Sayla — learn a language by talking to an AI coach that phones you. |
| Who's the audience? | Adults with a long streak in a tap-based app who still freeze when someone speaks to them. |
| What action? | Start the first call. |
| What real result can you show? | None claimed. Nothing in this deck is a metric, a testimonial or a before/after — every line is a description of the product or a description of the reader's experience. |

## Structure

1. Cover — streak vs. real world
2. Apps taught you to tap
3. Nobody taught you to answer
4. So Sayla calls you
5. The first call is uncomfortable (objection handling)
6. Ten minutes, no screen (the offer, concretely)
7. CTA — speak first, first call free

Slide 2 is deliberately the weakest link in the chain and the first candidate to cut when testing.

## Score — 17/20

| Criterion | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hook stops the scroll | 4/4 | Specific, universal, and slightly insulting in a way people send to friends |
| Slide 2 earns the next swipe | 3/4 | "Apps taught you to tap" restates the hook; it doesn't escalate |
| Promise delivered by the end | 4/4 | The deck explains the mechanism, not just the benefit |
| One CTA, unmissable | 4/4 | Single action, single destination |
| Visual system holds | 2/4 | Consistent, but every slide is the same layout — slide 5 could carry an image or a phone motif to break the rhythm |

## Captions

**TikTok / Reels**
> the streak was never the skill

**Instagram**
> You can hold a 900-day streak and still freeze when someone actually talks to you.
> Tapping is recognition. Speaking is recall, in real time, with a person waiting.
> That's the part nobody practises. Sayla calls you and waits for an answer.
> First call is free — link in bio.

**LinkedIn / X**
> Most language apps optimise for the tap. The tap is easy to measure and easy to score.
> The thing people actually want — holding a conversation — is slower, messier, and impossible to grade in a streak.
> We built Sayla around the uncomfortable part: a voice on the phone that asks you something and then waits.
